# Hello World. I'm Starting Over.

Hey, I'm Marcin.

Years ago, I wanted to get into cybersecurity. I started learning penetration testing, made plans, bought training materials — the usual enthusiastic beginning. Then work got interesting, other opportunities came along, and those plans kept moving down the list.

I don't regret how things turned out. I've worked on projects and had experiences I wouldn't trade. But the interest in hacking never really went away. It just spent a long time waiting for a convenient moment.

Recently, with my 40th birthday approaching, I was watching a Jimmy Carr stand-up show when a thought crossed my mind: **why not just do it for fun?**

I don't need to change careers or collect another certificate. I can spend an evening figuring out how something works simply because I enjoy it. Somehow, that took longer to work out than it should have.

So here we are. Back to pentesting, and giving this blog a fresh start.

## Where I'm Starting From

I've had two attempts at preparing for the OSCP, across two versions of the course. Both times, work brought projects and opportunities that were hard to turn down. The course materials are still there, including some I haven't finished going through.

These days, I'm spending some of my free time on Hack The Box and working through HTB Academy material. I'm also making my way through the Dante Pro Lab, where I've captured my first two flags without hints or walkthroughs.

Two flags. Not exactly a reason to update my LinkedIn headline, but **I'm pretty damn happy about them.**

That feeling of being stuck, trying something else, and finally understanding what you missed is a big part of why I came back. I'd like to keep doing that — and keep a useful record of what I learn.

## What You'll Find Here

ShellThisBox is where I'll write about machines, techniques, tools, and the things that take a while to click. Some posts will be full walkthroughs. Others might cover one enumeration step, a Linux privilege escalation technique, or a small detail that cost me an entire evening.

The rule I want to stick to is simple: **if I write about a technique, I want to understand it well enough to explain why it works.**

A command and a screenshot only tell part of the story. What made me run that command? Which part of the output mattered? What did I try next, and why? That's the part I find useful when reading someone else's notes, so that's what I'll try to include in mine.

I'll test what I describe, link to sources, and correct mistakes when I find them. Some articles will take longer because I need to go back and check something. That's fine; this is a hobby, and I'd like it to stay enjoyable.

There's also a practical benefit to putting my notes here: I might finally remember where I left them.

## Independent, Hinted, or Guided?

Each Hack The Box walkthrough will carry one of three labels:

- **Independent** — I completed the machine without hints, walkthroughs, or solution guides.
- **Guided** — I used an existing walkthrough or guide as part of the learning process.

If I needed help, I'll say so. There are people who know a hell of a lot more about pentesting than I do, and learning from their work is a sensible use of my time.

When I follow a guide, I still want to reproduce the steps, understand the reasoning, and explain the technique in my own words. I'll link to the sources I used so you can read their explanation too.

The label gives you some context for what you're reading. It also gives me a way to look back and see my progress. My first Independent walkthrough will be worth celebrating. Honestly, a Hinted one will feel pretty good too.

## Why the Fresh Start?

There were already a few articles on this site. I decided to take them down because I now have a clearer idea of what I want to build here.

I want to be able to come back to a post months later and follow my own reasoning. Ideally, someone else learning pentesting will be able to do the same, including seeing where I got stuck or followed the wrong lead.

Starting again gives me room to write that way from the beginning. The old articles were part of figuring it out.

## A Word About AI

I used AI to help build the Hugo template for this site, and I'll use it to help with writing, checking explanations, and working through ideas. It's useful to have something to bounce a problem off when you're learning on your own.

I still need to test the commands and understand the explanation before putting my name under it. A confident answer can be wrong, and I've had a fairly memorable demonstration of that.

I once told an AI that the weather was nice and it would be a good day to wash my car. It suggested walking to the car wash.

I'm still trying to work out how the car was supposed to get there. :)

So yes, I'll use AI. I'll also check its work — especially when transport is involved.

## One Machine at a Time

For now, I'm focusing on retired, easy Linux machines on Hack The Box. I want to spend time on manual enumeration, follow the clues, and understand how the pieces fit together before moving on.

The first walkthrough is already here: [Lame (Hack The Box)](/writeups/htb-lame/). It's Guided, and it gives me a starting point to build on.

I don't have a publishing schedule or a deadline for becoming good at this. I've got a hobby I kept putting off, a couple of flags I'm pleased with, and somewhere to write about what happens next.

If you're learning too, have a question, or spot something I got wrong, come say hello on [X (@shellthisbox)](https://x.com/shellthisbox). You can follow along for new posts and whatever I manage to figure out next. I'd be happy to hear what you're working on, too.

Let's see where this goes.

— Marcin
